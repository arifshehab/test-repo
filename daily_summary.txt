Per user follow-up questioning Norway's status, ran a dedicated Norwegian-language search and found a real, previously-missed development that this tracker had twice reported incorrectly. On July 22, 2026 -- one day BEFORE USTR's final determination -- Norway's Ministry of Trade, Industry and Fisheries formally announced a trade ban on products made with forced labour ("Innforer handelsforbud mot produkter fremstilt ved tvangsarbeid"), covering import, export and sale of goods made wholly or partly with forced labour including child labour. Trade Minister Cecilie Myrseth: "Tvangsarbeid er selvfolgelig forbudt i Norge i dag, og gjennom dette regelverket innforer vi ogsa et forbud mot import av varer fremstilt ved tvangsarbeid." Implementation is via incorporation of EU Forced Labour Regulation 2024/3015 into Norwegian law through the EEA Agreement, in force no later than December 14, 2027 (simultaneous with the EU), with the government committing to that date even if formal EEA incorporation runs late. Confirmed against the Norwegian government's own site (regjeringen.no) plus NRK (public broadcaster), NTB (national wire, via FriFagbevegelse), and NordiskPost. Norway reclassified No -> Partial: this is a formal government decision with a binding date, but the implementing Norwegian instrument has not been enacted yet, so it does not meet the Yes bar (directly parallel to Israel's cabinet decision, also Partial).
